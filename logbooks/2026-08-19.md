### Matching validation

For every visible pair, `sparse_pixel_matched_noise` and `add_stroke` matched both changed-pixel count and pixel MAE exactly or to stored integer precision. The sparse pixels were distributed outside the coherent reference-stroke region.

### Pilot diagnostics

| Crowding | Add > sparse top-10% | Add > sparse reference region | Median localization lift | Median reference enrichment | No-change max |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 100% | 100% | 12.40× | 2.05× | 2.98e-7 |
| 5 | 0% | 100% | 11.52× | 5.54× | 3.58e-7 |

The representation preserved the coherent stroke in the correct spatial region under both blank and moderate crowding. Sparse random pixels generated larger fixed top-10% responses under crowding because the same pixel budget was dispersed across far more patch locations. This was treated as a robustness finding rather than evidence that the coherent line disappeared.

## Design freeze

Before the formal run, the primary criteria were frozen around paired reference-region separation, localization lift, reference-region enrichment, no-change sanity, and spatial agreement. Fixed top-10%, dense noise, and global-token behavior remained mandatory secondary diagnostics.

### Formal interpretation

All frozen primary thresholds passed. The action-region response remained strongly localized under blank and moderate clutter, and the unrequired crowding-15 stress test also remained strong.

Absolute response magnitude declined with crowding. Mean added-stroke reference-region distance fell from approximately 0.746 at crowding 0 to 0.310 at crowding 5 and 0.218 at crowding 15. Global-token distance fell more sharply, from approximately 0.359 to 0.049 and 0.024. This supports using spatial patch tokens rather than a global token as the Gate 2 target.

The fixed top-10% statistic behaved poorly under clutter because the sparse control spread matched pixels over about four times as many patch cells as the coherent line. This predicted limitation is reported and will inform the loss design; it does not override the preregistered action-region criteria.

## Gate decision

**Gate 1: PASS.**

The frozen representation contains enough local information about one-stroke consequences to justify training a deterministic action-conditioned one-step predictor. This does not yet establish predictability, baseline superiority, candidate ranking, or planning.

## Next step

Write the Gate 2 protocol, generate independent train/validation/test transitions, and compare no-change, mean-delta, linear, and small nonlinear residual predictors. Do not begin reinforcement learning or multi-step planning.
