## Version-2 paired smoke test

Configuration:

- model: `facebook/dinov2-small`
- samples: 3
- crowding: 0 and 5
- patch grid: 16×16
- paired across crowding: yes
- canonical stroke: black, width 2, minimum normalized length 0.35
- comparison pairs: 42
- tests: 6 passed

### Findings

- No-change distances remained numerical zero.
- Added strokes localized strongly:
  - crowding 0 median localization lift was about `16.9×`,
  - crowding 5 median localization lift was about `7.8×`.
- Mean changed-region enrichment was about `2.1×` at crowding 0 and `6.0×` at crowding 5.
- The crowded heatmap remained concentrated around the new stroke even as the global-token response weakened.
- The added stroke beat dense pixel-MAE-matched noise on top-10% patch distance in `3/3` blank samples and `0/3` five-stroke samples.

### Control diagnosis

The dense matched control preserved total pixel MAE but changed approximately 46–53% of pixels, while the coherent stroke changed about 1–2%. It touched nearly every patch and was not support-matched. The result therefore demonstrates dense-noise sensitivity but is not a clean line-versus-random-structure comparison.

### Decision

Version 2 strengthened evidence that frozen spatial features preserve the new stroke's location under moderate clutter. It did not make a Gate 1 decision because the primary nuisance control still differed drastically in spatial support and the sample count was only three.

## Final pre-run adjustment

Added:

- `sparse_pixel_matched_noise`, which exactly matches both changed-pixel count and total absolute pixel difference while scattering changes outside the coherent stroke region,

The dense control remains as a stress test. The sparse matched control is now the frozen primary gate comparison.
