# Thesis-ready ranking-aware latent result

A frozen task-autoencoder representation initially supported only weak exact-action retrieval when the transition predictor was trained with balanced latent residual MSE. On an untouched formal test set, the matched MSE-only predictor retrieved the correct post-action latent from four counterfactual candidates in 31.44% of cases, only modestly above the 25% chance rate. Replacing the training objective with a validation-selected combination of balanced MSE and counterfactual ranking loss increased retrieval to 74.44%, an absolute gain of 43.00 percentage points, while increasing action-region MSE by only 1.56% relative to MSE-only. The ranking-aware model retained 70.33% and 68.24% error reductions relative to identity and mean-delta baselines, respectively, and passed all preregistered primary criteria across three model seeds.

These findings indicate that the task latent did not simply discard stroke information. Instead, the earlier failure was partly caused by an objective mismatch: average residual regression did not enforce the local ordering needed to distinguish the executed action from similar width, intensity, and position alternatives. Explicit counterfactual ranking made this information operational for action discrimination. Secondary tests showed strong gains for an unseen width and unseen intensities, with smaller positive gains under crowding 30 and 60; absolute retrieval fell to 43% at crowding 60, identifying heavy canvas crowding as a limitation.

The conclusion should be scoped to the tested 64×64 grayscale renderer, straight opaque strokes, frozen 16×16×32 task-autoencoder latent, and 19,232-parameter action-conditioned MLP. The method is JEPA-inspired but is not presented as a canonical JEPA implementation.
